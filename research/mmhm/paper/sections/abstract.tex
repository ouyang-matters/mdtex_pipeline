\begin{abstract}
We maintain the Betti numbers over $\Z$ of a simplicial complex of dimension at
most three under insertions and deletions of simplices. Version~1 of this paper
proposed a localised update of a boundary matrix reduced by discrete Morse
theory; that update is not exact, and this version replaces it. For each
dimension we maintain a reduced matrix $R=BV$ together with the change of
basis~$V$. An inserted simplex is reduced against the existing pivots; a
deleted simplex is removed from every basis chain that contains it, by adding
either a cycle or the column with the smallest pivot, which is the rank-only
form of known updates for zigzag persistence and warm-started reductions. We
prove that the Betti numbers are exact after every edit, for every order of the
cells. Discrete Morse theory enters only through that order: if cells are
ordered by reverse removal time in a collapse, every gradient pair is an
apparent pair, so a rebuild makes column additions only in the columns of
critical cells, where, after following the gradient flow, it performs the
reduction of the Morse complex. The cost of
an edit is output-sensitive, and we prove that it admits no bound in terms of
the size of the edit. On five workload families with up to \NumSizeMax{}
simplices, all \NumReportedStates{} Betti vectors reported by exact methods
agreed with independent oracles. Where edits create and destroy only small
cycles, in refined and carved 3-balls and in flag complexes, the update latency
depends little on the size of the complex; at \NumWTwoNThirtyThreeSEightSize{}
simplices it is \NumWTwoNThirtyThreeSEightMmhmSpeedupFullId{} times lower than
that of static recomputation. Where edits create or destroy cycles that span the
complex, the cost follows those cycles: on punctured surfaces the method is at
most \NumWOneSphereLFiveMmhmSpeedupFullId{} times faster on average, its tail
latency is worse than recomputation's, and combinatorial counters are far
cheaper; on non-locality witnesses its cost grows linearly with the complex, as
the theory predicts. A reimplementation of the rule of version~1 reports wrong Betti numbers
after \NumLcrErrAll\,\% of the updates.
\end{abstract}
