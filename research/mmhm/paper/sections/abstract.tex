\begin{abstract}
We study the maintenance of the Betti numbers over $\Z$ of a simplicial complex
under insertions and deletions of simplices. For each dimension we maintain a
reduced matrix $R=BV$ together with the change of basis~$V$, a dynamic reduced
basis. An inserted simplex is reduced against the existing pivots; a deleted
simplex is removed from every basis chain that contains it, by adding either a
cycle or the column with the smallest pivot, the rank-only form of known updates
for zigzag persistence and warm-started reductions. We prove that the Betti
numbers are exact after every edit, for every order of the cells. In the order
given by a collapse, every gradient pair is an apparent pair, and a build
performs column additions only in the columns of critical cells, where they
coincide with the reduction of the Morse complex. The cost of an edit is
output-sensitive, and on explicit families every toggle of a single simplex
costs $\Omega(n)$, so no bound in terms of the size of an edit holds. On five
workload families with up to \NumSizeMax{} simplices, the method reported the
correct Betti numbers in every checked state. Where edits create and destroy
only small cycles, its latency depends little on the size of the complex: on
\NumWTwoNThirtyThreeSEightSize{} simplices it is
\NumMainWTwoLargestSpeedupMin--\NumMainWTwoLargestSpeedupMax{} times faster than
the better static recomputation, a gain due to the dynamic basis itself.
Growth-triggered rebuilds and the Morse order add further gains where edits
change the topology and the basis fills in. Where edits create cycles that span
the complex, the cost grows linearly, as the lower bound predicts, and the tail
latency can exceed that of recomputation.
\end{abstract}
