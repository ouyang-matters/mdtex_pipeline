\begin{abstract}
We study the maintenance of the Betti numbers over $\Z$ of a simplicial complex
under insertions and deletions of simplices. For each dimension we maintain a
reduced matrix $R=BV$ together with the change of basis~$V$, a dynamic reduced
basis. An inserted simplex is reduced against the existing pivots; a deleted
simplex is removed from every basis chain that contains it, by adding either a
cycle or the column with the smallest pivot, the rank-only form of known updates
for zigzag persistence and warm-started reductions. We prove that the Betti
numbers are exact after every edit, for every order of the cells. In the order
given by a collapse, every gradient pair is an apparent pair, and a build
performs column additions only in the columns of critical cells, where they
coincide with the reduction of the Morse complex. The cost of an edit is
output-sensitive, and we exhibit families on which every toggle of a single
simplex costs $\Omega(n)$, so no bound in terms of the size of an edit holds. On
five workload families with up to \NumSizeMax{} simplices, every Betti vector
reported by an exact method was checked against a reference computation and none
disagreed. Where edits create and destroy only small cycles, in refined and
carved 3-balls and in flag complexes, the latency hardly depends on the size of
the complex: at \NumWTwoNThirtyThreeSEightSize{} simplices every variant is
\NumWTwoNThirtyThreeSEightDrbSpeedupFullIdMin--\NumWTwoNThirtyThreeSEightDrbSpeedupFullIdMax{}
times faster than static recomputation, with or without the Morse order. On
punctured surfaces the method is at most \NumWOneSphereLFiveMmhmSpeedupFullId{}
times faster on average and has the worse tail latency, and combinatorial
counters are far cheaper; on the non-locality witnesses its cost grows linearly
with the complex. The Morse order saves nothing at build time against
structured insertion orders but much against a random one, and helps between
rebuilds only on some workloads.
\end{abstract}
