\section{Erratum to version 1}\label{sec:erratum}

This paper replaces version~1~\cite{ouyang2025mmhm_v1}. The update rule of
version~1 does not maintain exact Betti numbers. We state what version~1
specified, give instances on which it fails although none of its safeguards
applies, and record an inconsistency in its experiment P2. Section and figure
numbers below refer to version~1.

\paragraph{What version 1 specified.}
An acyclic matching on $K$ defines critical cells, critical chain groups
$C_k^C$ and a critical boundary $\bd_k^C$ ``induced by the matching via
$V$-paths (Forman's gradient flow)'', with matrices $\Bm{k}^C$. Betti numbers
are read off from $r_k=\rank\Bm{k}^C$ and the numbers $n_k^C$ of critical cells
(\S2.2, \S2.5). The matching is computed by a discrete Morse reduction at
initialisation and again at \emph{recompression events} (\S2.2), which are
triggered by any of: the periodic trigger $t\equiv0\pmod m$; the locality
trigger $\rho_t\ge\tau$, where
\[
  \rho_t=\frac{\sum_k|R_k^C(t)|}{\max\bigl(1,\sum_k|C_k^C(t)|\bigr)},
\]
$C_k^C(t)$ being the critical $k$-cells before update $t$ and
$R_k^C(t)\subseteq C_k^C(t)$ those ``whose column incidences change due to
update $t$''; and a validity trigger when a precondition of a combinatorial gate
fails. Typical values are $m=32$ and $\tau=0.30$. The gates are optional
(``No combinatorial gate is used in P2'', \S3.3). No rule is given for updating
the matching between recompressions. For an edit, $R_k\subseteq\Kc{k}$ is the set
of $k$-simplices ``whose incidences in the boundary operators are affected by
the modification'', computed ``via a face-difference procedure that compares
the pre-update and post-update stars of the modified region'', and the affected
critical columns are
\[
  R_k^C=\{\,j : \sigma_j^k\in C_k^C\ \text{and}\ \sigma_j^k\in R_k\,\}
\]
(\S2.3). The columns $\{\Bm{k}^C[:,j] : j\in R_k^C\}$ are replaced by their new
incidence patterns, ``followed by reapplying column-reduction operations only to
these affected columns and to any columns whose pivots conflict with them'';
``when a column is replaced, its pivot assignment is cleared, the column is
re-reduced against existing pivots, and any resulting pivot conflicts are
propagated in a localized manner''. Two further clauses concern rows and
ranks. \S2.3: ``If the update inserts or deletes $(k{-}1)$-simplices (thus
changing the row set of $\Bm{k}^C$), the pivot map of $\Bm{k}^C$ is
re-initialized for that dimension.'' \S2.5: $r_k$ is recomputed ``if either the
column set of $\Bm{k}^C$ changes ($R_k^C\ne\emptyset$) or the row set of
$\Bm{k}^C$ changes'', and ``if a localized reducer is available in dimension
$k^*$, the rank $r_{k^*}$ is updated incrementally by reassigning pivots in the
column-reduced form (over $\Z$), avoiding any operation on unaffected columns''.
Version~1 states that its rule ``ensures that the rank of $\Bm{k}^C$ is updated
exactly'' (\S2.3) and ``guarantees exact Betti numbers'' (\S2.5). Both
statements are false, for two independent reasons, E1 and E2.

\paragraph{What counts as a counterexample.}
An instance refutes version~1 as specified only if none of its safeguards
applies. All instances below have at most four updates, so the periodic trigger
does not fire. For the locality trigger we bound $\rho_t$ from above with the
most generous numerator that any local reading of $R_k^C(t)$ allows: every cell
that is critical before \emph{or} after update $t$ and lies in the closed star of
a vertex of an edited simplex, before or after the update; the denominator is
the number of critical cells before the update, as defined above. We require
this bound to be below $\tau=0.30$ at every update. The gate preconditions
(connected $2$-manifolds) fail on these complexes, so with gates enabled the
validity trigger would request a recompression; the instances therefore use
version~1 without gates, which is how it ran P2. Finally, we
check each instance against the row and rank clauses quoted above.

\paragraph{E1: a reduced column records the columns it absorbed.}
In a column reduction $R=BV$, a reduced column $R_b=\bd b+\bd a_1+\dots$ is
correct only while the absorbed columns $a_i$ are present with their old
boundaries. The rule above re-reduces a replaced column $a$, but leaves every
$R_b$ that absorbed $a$ in place. Such an $R_b$ need not own a pivot (it may be
zero), so it is never in conflict with anything, and nothing in the rule
revisits it. A deletion is a replacement by the zero column and fails in the
same way. The DRB repairs exactly this: $\occ(a)$ lists every slot whose $V$
contains $a$, and $\Del$ rewrites all of them (Theorem~\ref{thm:exact}(c)).

\begin{example}[E1, matrix form]\label{ex:e1-matrix}
Rows $e_1<e_2$, $B=[\,a\mid b\,]$ with $a=b=e_1$. Reduction: $R_a=e_1$ owns pivot
$e_1$; $R_b=b+a=0$; rank $1$. Replace $a$ by $e_2$: its pivot is cleared, it is
re-reduced to $R_a=e_2$, which owns the free pivot $e_2$, so there is no
conflict; $R_b=0$ is not touched. The reported rank is $1$; the rank of
$[\,e_2\mid e_1\,]$ is $2$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[E1 for version 1 as specified]\label{ex:e1-simplicial}
Let $K_0$ consist of \RefEOneVOnePad{} isolated vertices $0,1,\dots$. Every
matching on $K_0$ is empty, since $K_0$ has no pair of incident cells, so the
Morse reduction at initialisation leaves every cell critical. Version~1 gives no
rule for matching inserted cells; we keep them critical, so $\Bm{k}^C=\Bm{k}$
throughout and the localized reducer acts on $\Bm{1}$ itself. Apply four
updates of one edit each, with rows and columns in index order:
\[
  t=1{:}\ {+}\{0,1\},\qquad t=2{:}\ {+}\{0,2\},\qquad t=3{:}\ {+}\{1,2\},\qquad
  t=4{:}\ {-}\{0,1\}.
\]
After $t=3$ the reduction gives $R_{01}=\{0,1\}$, $R_{02}=\{0,2\}$ and
$R_{12}=\{1,2\}+\{0,2\}+\{0,1\}=0$. At $t=4$ the column of $\{0,1\}$ releases its
pivot and is dropped, and $R_{12}=0$ stays. The reported Betti numbers are
$(\beta_0,\beta_1)=(19,1)$; the truth is $(18,0)$, because $\{0,2\}$ and
$\{1,2\}$ form a path. The DRB has $V_{12}=\{\{1,2\},\{0,2\},\{0,1\}\}$, so
$\occ(\{0,1\})$ contains the slots of $\{0,1\}$ and $\{1,2\}$; Case A sets
$V_{01}\gets\{\{1,2\},\{0,2\}\}$ (still $\bd V_{01}=\{0,1\}=R_{01}$), removes the
slot of $\{1,2\}$, and reports $(18,0)$.
\emph{Safeguards.} The bound on $\rho_t$ is $0.15$, $0.24$, $0.27$ and $0.26$
for $t=1,\dots,4$ (at most \RefEOneVOneRhoMax). No update inserts or deletes a
vertex, and no vertex changes its critical status, so neither the literal
row-set clause nor a broader reading of it applies to $\Bm{1}^C$. The failure
does not depend on the order of the rows $0,1,2$: it occurs for
\RefEOneVOneRowOrders{} of the 6 orders.
\emph{The rank clause.} If ``$r_k$ is recomputed'' in \S2.5 is read as a
recomputation of $\rank\Bm{1}^C$ from scratch whenever a column of $\Bm{1}^C$
changes, then E1 does not occur, but every such update is then a full rank
computation of $\Bm{1}^C$. Version~1 presents the localized reducer, which
updates $r_k$ ``incrementally by reassigning pivots'', as its method; E1 refutes
that reducer. E2 below fails under every reading.
\end{example}

Without the padding, the same seven edits ($+\{0\},+\{1\},+\{2\}$ followed by
the four updates above) are the shortest edit sequence on which the rule fails.
An exhaustive enumeration of the valid edit sequences on at most four
vertices, up to relabelling (\RefEOneCountsShort{} sequences of lengths 1 to 6,
and \RefEOneCountSeven{} of length 7), finds no failure of length at most 6 and
\RefEOneFailSeven{} of length 7; a sequence of length at most 6 on five or more
vertices contains at most one edit of positive dimension and cannot fail. On so
small a complex, however, $\rho_t$ exceeds $\tau$, which is why the instance above
is padded.

\paragraph{E2: the affected set misses distant critical columns.}
The critical boundary is a sum over gradient paths, $\dM c=\flow(\bd c)$
(Section~\ref{sec:morse}), and it depends on every pair along every gradient
path leaving the facets of $c$. These paths can be arbitrarily long, so an edit
to a matched cell can change $\dM c$ for critical cells $c$ far from the edit,
while $R_k^C$ contains only critical cells whose own incidences change. Since
version~1 does not say how the matching is maintained between recompressions, we
use the least intrusive convention: keep every pair that does not involve a
deleted cell; the partner of a deleted matched cell, and every inserted cell,
becomes critical. The restriction of an acyclic matching is acyclic, so this is
a valid matching. (Under this convention insertions are harmless: a new cell is
maximal and critical and lies on no gradient path. Deleting a matched cell is
not.) In the two examples below the stored column of the distant critical cell
is never replaced, so the error is the same under the three readings of the row
and rank clauses: re-initialising the pivot map rebuilds it from the stored,
stale columns; recomputing $r_k$ computes the rank of the stored, stale matrix;
and the incremental reducer only inserts the column of the new critical cell.

\begin{example}[E2 on a graph]\label{ex:e2-cycle}
Let $K_0$ be two disjoint $8$-cycles, on the vertices $0,\dots,7$ and
$8,\dots,15$, with the collapse matching of Definition~\ref{def:collapse}. Its
critical cells are the edges $c=\{0,1\}$ and $\{8,9\}$ and the vertices
$r=\{5\}$ and $\{13\}$, and $\dM c=r+r=0$: the gradient paths from the two
endpoints of $c$ are the two arcs of the first cycle from those endpoints to
$r$. At $t=1$ delete the matched edge $y=\{2,3\}$, whose partner is $\{2\}$. Now
$\{2\}$ is critical, the arc through $y$ ends at $\{2\}$, and
$\dM c=\{2\}+\{5\}$. The edge $c$ shares no vertex with $y$, so its column is not
in $R_1^C$ and stays $0$. The reported Betti numbers are $(3,2)$; the truth is
$(2,1)$.
\emph{Safeguards.} The closed stars of the vertices $2$ and $3$ contain no
critical cell before the update, and after it only $\{2\}$, so
$\rho_1\le1/4=\RefETwoCycleRho$. No vertex is inserted or deleted, so the literal
row-set clause does not apply to $\Bm{1}^C$; under a broader reading the new
critical row $\{2\}$ re-initialises the pivot map of $\Bm{1}^C$, which leaves the
stored column of $c$ at $0$.
More generally, on a cycle $C_L$ the collapse leaves one critical edge $c$ and
one critical vertex $r$, the two arcs from the endpoints of $c$ to $r$ contain
every matched edge exactly once, and deleting any matched edge $y=\mu(x)$ makes
$\dM c=r+x\ne0$; when $y$ avoids $c$ and $r$, version~1 does not see the change.
A second component (or padding by isolated vertices) is needed only to keep
$\rho_t$ below $\tau$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[E2 with a critical triangle]\label{ex:e2-torus}
Let $K_0$ be the $5\times5$ grid torus on the vertices $1,\dots,25$, in which
vertex $(i,j)$, $i,j\in\mathbb{Z}/5$, is labelled $1+5i+j$ and the triangles are
\[
  \{(i,j),(i{+}1,j),(i{+}1,j{+}1)\},\qquad \{(i,j),(i,j{+}1),(i{+}1,j{+}1)\},
\]
together with the cone with apex $0$ over the meridian $1$--$6$--$11$--$16$--$21$;
$\beta(K_0)=(1,1,1)$. The collapse matching (first-in-first-out queue) has the
critical cells $\{0,1,6\}$, $\{1,2,7\}$, $\{4,10\}$, $\{15,16\}$ and $\{24\}$, and
$\dM\{1,2,7\}=0$. At $t=1$ delete the matched triangle $\{8,13,14\}$, paired with
the edge $\{8,13\}$. This changes $\dM\{1,2,7\}$ to $\{8,13\}$, although
$\{1,2,7\}$ and $\{8,13,14\}$ share no vertex. The reported Betti numbers are
$(1,2,1)$; the truth is $(1,1,0)$.
\emph{Safeguards.} The only cell that is critical before or after the update
and lies in the closed stars of $8$, $13$ and $14$ is $\{8,13\}$, so
$\rho_1\le1/5=\RefETwoTorusRho$. The update deletes a $2$-simplex, so the literal
row-set clause concerns $\Bm{3}^C$, which has no columns; under a broader
reading the new critical row $\{8,13\}$ re-initialises the pivot map of
$\Bm{2}^C$, which leaves the stored column of $\{1,2,7\}$ at $0$.
\end{example}

Smaller complexes show the same change of $\dM c$, for instance a single
$4$-cycle or a $3\times3$ torus with a disk, but on them $\rho_1\ge\tau$ under
the counting above, so version~1 would recompress and return correct Betti
numbers. They illustrate the defect of the affected set but do not refute
version~1 as specified.

\paragraph{The ablation \method{lcr}.}
The method \method{lcr} of the experiments (\cref{sec:exp-methods}) applies
version~1's re-reduction rule to the full boundary matrices, without basis
tracking. It isolates defect~E1 and is not a reimplementation of version~1: it
forms no Morse complex (so E2 cannot occur), never recompresses, and has no
gates. Its error rates therefore measure E1, not version~1 as a whole. In
particular, on the connected surfaces of W1, gates of the kind version~1
describes would return correct Betti numbers (Remark~\ref{rem:surfaces}) where
\method{lcr} is wrong, and a periodic recompression would restore a correct
state every $m$ updates, after which errors can occur again. We did not run a
variant with recompression or gates, so we make no quantitative claim about
version~1 itself.

\paragraph{P2: an inconsistent workload description.}
Version~1, \S3.3, defines the P2 sequence as alternating a refinement and ``its
exact inverse'', $\textsc{Refine}(R_t)$ followed by $\textsc{Coarsen}(R_t)$. Under
that definition $|K_t|$ is bounded and returns to $|K_0|$ after every
coarsening. The same section states that each step injects $O(1)$ new
simplices, ``thus $|C_t|=\Theta(t)$'', and Figures~4 and~5 of version~1 show
sizes growing linearly over 500 updates. The two descriptions cannot hold for
the same sequence, and version~1 does not determine which sequence produced its
P2 timings. The workload W2 of this paper lets the size perform a random walk
around its initial value and records it.

\paragraph{What the experiments of version 1 checked.}
Version~1 shows Betti traces for P1 and P3 (its Figures~2(b) and~7(b)) and
states that they match the specification (P1) and agree across methods (P3).
Both are connected surfaces with
known connectivity, whose Betti numbers follow from $\chi$, $\beta_0=1$ and the
boundary-edge criterion without any matrix (Remark~\ref{rem:surfaces}), the
formulas behind version~1's gates; version~1 does not state whether its gates
were enabled there. For P2, the one experiment run without gates, version~1
reports no Betti numbers and does not compare its output with an independent
computation. Such a comparison would have exposed E1 even though the Betti
numbers of P2 are constant: on the analogous workload W2, \method{lcr} reports
wrong Betti numbers after almost every update, for the first time at update
\NumLcrWTwoNFifteenSEightFirstWrong{} (\cref{sec:e0}, \cref{tab:correctness}). In this paper every exact method is compared with independent
recomputations (\cref{sec:exp-correctness}).

\paragraph{Cost claims.}
Version~1, \S2.6, states a local reduction cost of
$\Theta\bigl(\sum_k (s_k^C)^2\bigr)$ ``under sparse operations with limited
fill-in''. That analysis concerns the rule shown above to be inexact, and we
make no statement about it. For the exact method of this paper,
Proposition~\ref{prop:nonlocal} shows that no bound in terms of the size of the
update holds, and we report costs as measurements.
