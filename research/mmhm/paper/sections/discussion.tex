\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Coefficients.}
Every result of this paper is over $\Z$. Version~1 discussed integer
coefficients, torsion and the Smith normal form, and described its update as
working over a chosen coefficient ring; nothing in it supports these statements,
and we withdraw them. Ranks over a field do not see torsion, which requires the
Smith normal form over the integers~\cite{munkres1984elements}, and Betti
numbers over $\Z$ can differ from those over other fields: the real projective
plane has Betti numbers $(1,1,1)$ over $\Z$ and $(1,0,0)$ over the
rationals~\cite{hatcher2002algebraic}. We have neither implemented nor tested
other coefficients.

\paragraph{Dimension.}
The proofs of \cref{sec:theory} hold in every dimension, but the engine and all
experiments are restricted to complexes of dimension at most three.

\paragraph{No locality theorem.}
\Cref{prop:nonlocal} shows that on a cycle graph and on closed surfaces every
toggle of a single simplex costs the dynamic reduced basis $\Omega(n)$
operations, because (I3) forces it to store a cycle of $n$ cells when that cycle
appears. The cost of an update is therefore bounded only in output-sensitive
terms (\cref{prop:cost}): by footprints and reduction chains, which depend on the
whole history and on the order. Whether these stay small is an empirical
question for each workload, which E6 and E8 measure.

\paragraph{Surfaces.}
For a connected surface whose connectivity and surface property are known in
advance, all Betti numbers follow from a few counters maintained in $O(1)$ per
edit (\cref{rem:surfaces}). Such workloads do not exercise the algebra of a
homology-maintenance method, and on them the counters, not linear algebra, are
the method of choice. W1 is included to measure what the algebra costs where it
is not needed and because closed surfaces exhibit the worst case of
\cref{prop:nonlocal}. \pending{E5: latency of \method{euler} relative to the
DRB methods on W1}

\paragraph{Rebuild policy.}
The growth threshold $\alpha$ and the period $m$ are heuristics without a
guarantee. \Cref{lem:apparent} and its corollaries describe the state right
after a rebuild only (\cref{rem:morse-scope}); between rebuilds, inserted cells
receive the largest keys and deletions make $V$ non-triangular, and we make no
sparsity claim. A rebuild is a collapse followed by a static recomputation
with $V$ tracked, so whether it pays off depends on how much sparsity it
restores and for how long. \pending{E7 and E8: whether rebuilding in the Morse order pays
off against \method{drb-id-rb}, \method{mmhm-norb} and \method{drb-id}, and how
sensitive this is to $\alpha$ and $m$}

\paragraph{Measurements.}
All timings come from one virtual machine, with single-threaded runs and one
compiler setting; absolute times will differ on other machines. The counts of
column additions, the footprints and $\nnz(R)+\nnz(V)$ do not depend on the
machine, and we report them next to the times. \method{gudhi} runs through its
Python interface and computes persistence pairs, which are more than Betti
numbers; its times are a reference point for a widely used static library, not
the best possible static recomputation. The dynamic reduced basis stores $V$
and the footprint index, which a ranks-only static recomputation does not need.

\paragraph{W4.}
The flag complexes of W4 are truncated at dimension $D$, so $\beta_D$ is
dominated by unfilled boundaries of $(D+2)$-cliques
(\cref{sec:exp-workloads}). W4 measures the algebra on non-manifold complexes in
which every Betti number varies; it says nothing about the topology of point
clouds.

\subsection{When to use what}\label{sec:when}

\paragraph{Barcodes.} If the persistence of features across the sequence is
needed, zigzag persistence~\cite{carlsson2010zigzag} is the right tool:
$\beta_k(K_t)$ is the number of $k$-dimensional intervals of the zigzag barcode
that contain~$t$, so zigzag
algorithms~\cite{carlsson2009zigzag,milosavljevic2011zigzag,maria2024discrete,dey2022fast}
answer our query as well, and the incremental algorithm of Carlsson, de Silva
and Morozov~\cite{carlsson2009zigzag} does so after every update. We did not
compare against zigzag implementations; that comparison remains open.

\paragraph{Connectivity only.} If only $\beta_0$ is needed, or the complex is a
graph, for which $\beta_1=n_1-n_0+\beta_0$, fully dynamic connectivity maintains
$\beta_0$ in polylogarithmic amortised time per edge
edit~\cite{holm2001polylog}; on W5a, no method that stores cycle
representatives explicitly can match this (\cref{prop:nonlocal}).

\paragraph{Special structure.} For complexes in the $3$-sphere built by
insertions only, the algorithm of Delfinado and
Edelsbrunner~\cite{delfinado1995incremental} needs near-linear total time. For
connected surfaces with known connectivity, the counters of \cref{rem:surfaces}
need $O(1)$ per edit.

\paragraph{Rare queries or large batches.} If Betti numbers are needed only
occasionally, or updates touch a large part of the complex, static
recomputation with clearing (\method{full-id}) avoids storing $V$ and the
footprint index. \pending{E2: the edit size, if any, above which static
recomputation was faster on W2}

\paragraph{This method.} The dynamic reduced basis fits the remaining case:
exact Betti numbers after every update of a general complex of dimension at most
three, under arbitrary insertions and deletions, including non-manifold
complexes in which every Betti number changes. Its cost per update is bounded by
footprints and reduction chains, not by the size of the update.
