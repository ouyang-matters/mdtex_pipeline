\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have replaced the localised update of version~1, which is not exact, by a
dynamic reduced basis that maintains the $\Z$ Betti numbers of a simplicial
complex exactly under insertions and deletions of simplices. Insertion is the
standard reduction step; deletion corrects every basis chain that contains the
deleted simplex, which is the rank-only form of known zigzag and warm-start
updates. Exactness holds for every order of the cells. Discrete Morse theory
enters through the order chosen at a rebuild: ordering cells by reverse removal
time in a collapse makes every gradient pair an apparent pair and confines the
column additions of the rebuild to critical columns. The cost of an update is
bounded by footprints and reduction chains, and it cannot be bounded by the size
of the update.

The experiments agree with this picture. None of the \NumReportedStates{} Betti
vectors reported by exact methods was wrong, while
a reimplementation of the rule of version~1 was wrong after \NumLcrErrAll\,\% of
the updates. Where the edits touch only small cycles, the method's latency
hardly depends on the size of the complex, and on the largest W2 instance it is
\NumWTwoNThirtyThreeSEightMmhmSpeedupFullId{} times below that of static
recomputation. Where the edits create or destroy cycles that span the complex,
on surfaces and on the non-locality witnesses, the cost grows with those cycles,
the average gain over recomputation is at most a constant factor or negative,
and on the surfaces and the cycle graph the tail latency is worse; on surfaces
with known connectivity,
combinatorial counters are the better tool. Rebuilding in the Morse order pays
off when the basis grows and costs time when it does not, and the Morse order
itself gives a modest, workload-dependent reduction of the reduction work
between rebuilds.

The results are limited to $\Z$ coefficients, to complexes of dimension at most
three, to one machine and to single-threaded runs; no locality bound holds, and
the rebuild policy is a heuristic. Open questions are maintaining the matching
between rebuilds, so that the guarantees of \cref{lem:apparent} hold at all
times; rebuild policies with provable guarantees; bounds on footprints and
reduction chains for structured families of complexes and edits; other
coefficients; and a comparison with implementations of zigzag persistence.
