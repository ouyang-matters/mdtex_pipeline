\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have replaced the localised update of version~1, which is not exact, by a
dynamic reduced basis that maintains the $\Z$ Betti numbers of a simplicial
complex exactly under insertions and deletions of simplices. Insertion is the
standard reduction step; deletion corrects every basis chain that contains the
deleted simplex, which is the rank-only form of known zigzag and warm-start
updates. Exactness holds for every order of the cells. Discrete Morse theory
enters through the order chosen at a rebuild: ordering cells by reverse removal
time in a collapse makes every gradient pair an apparent pair, confines the
column additions of the rebuild to critical columns, and makes the reduction of
those columns coincide with the reduction of the Morse complex. The cost of an
update is bounded by footprints and reduction chains; it cannot be bounded by the
size of the update, and we exhibit families on which it is linear in the size of
the complex. The erratum gives concrete counterexamples to the rule of
version~1, and every exact method in our experiments was checked against three
independent oracles. \pending{two sentences with the main measured results of

Several questions remain open: maintaining the matching between rebuilds, so
that the guarantees of \cref{lem:apparent} hold at all times; rebuild policies
with provable guarantees; bounds on footprints and reduction chains for
structured families of complexes and edits; coefficients other than $\Z$; and a
comparison with implementations of zigzag persistence.
