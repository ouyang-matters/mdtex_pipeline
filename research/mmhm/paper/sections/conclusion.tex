\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Tracking the change of basis of a reduced boundary matrix makes it possible to
maintain the $\Z$ Betti numbers of a simplicial complex exactly under insertions
and deletions of simplices, for every order of the cells. Insertion is the
standard reduction step, and deletion is the rank-only form of known zigzag and
warm-start updates. A collapse order makes every gradient pair an apparent pair,
so that a build performs its column additions only in critical columns, where
they are exactly the reduction of the Morse complex. The cost of an update is
output-sensitive, and on explicit families it is linear in the size of the
complex for edits of size one.

Experimentally, the dynamic basis is exact on every state we checked and, where
edits create and destroy only small cycles, much cheaper than recomputation:
on our largest refinement instance every variant is
\NumMainWTwoLargestSpeedupMin--\NumMainWTwoLargestSpeedupMax{} times faster than
the better static baseline. This gain comes from the basis itself. Rebuilding
controls the growth of the basis where edits change the topology, and the Morse
order helps on some of these workloads; their combination is the fastest variant
on cavities. Where edits create cycles that span the complex, the work is
linear, as the theory predicts, and the method keeps at most a constant factor
over recomputation, with a heavier tail.

Open questions are maintaining the matching between rebuilds, rebuild policies
with guarantees, bounds on footprints and reduction chains for structured
families of complexes and edits, other coefficients, and comparisons with
dynamic algorithms such as zigzag persistence and warm-started factorisations.
